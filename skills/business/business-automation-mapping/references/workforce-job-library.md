# Workforce Job Library

The structure that lets one department definition serve every business, so the
owner stops re-explaining the same process per venture.

## The mechanism

**The department defines a job once. The company only declares whether it needs
it.** Company notes carry an allocation (needed / not needed / priority), never
a second copy of the procedure. This is what stops per-business duplication:
adding a business becomes a list of declarations, not a new set of playbooks.

A business that lacks a function simply switches those jobs off — a resort
skips production, a single-site cafe skips deals until it franchises.

## Department set

Seven owner-facing departments plus one shared floor:

| Department | Covers |
|---|---|
| Sales | targeting, lead sourcing, enrichment, outreach writing, sequencing |
| Marketing | insights, creation, repurposing, distribution, brand deals |
| Deals | reply handling, inbound, call cycle, deal artifacts, pipeline ops |
| Operations | onboarding, build ops, reliability, client comms, knowledge |
| Customer | support, success, community |
| Back Office | money in, reporting, finance ops, records, office, talent |
| Production/Commissary | demand planning, procurement, production, QA & food safety, dispatch, cost control |
| Intelligence *(shared floor)* | companies, people, markets, monitoring, synthesis |

Production/Commissary is **authored, not inherited** — knowledge-work
blueprints have no physical jobs. Intelligence is a floor that serves the other
seven; it gets no business-facing mandate.

## Autonomy tiers

Four tiers, and the fourth is the one frameworks omit:

- **solo** — agent runs it end to end
- **assisted** — agent drafts, human confirms
- **human** — human decides, agent prepares the inputs
- **physical** — human hands do it; the agent only logs time, yield,
  temperature, cost. Never counted as automated.

When adopting a published framework, assign tiers per job and then reconcile
your totals against the framework's published department counts. A mismatch
means you misread the framework.

## Allocation priorities

Priority comes from the owner's own bottleneck list, not from department order.
Mark P1 only for jobs that close a step the owner currently performs by hand;
physical steps can never be P1 because they cannot be automated at all — they
get instrumented instead.

## Ownership

Every department needs one named human owner and one named lead agent before
any worker agent is built. A department with no human owner produces reports
nobody acts on.
